     <p align="justify"><u><i><b>Harvard University</b></i></u> is a private Ivy League research university located in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and stands as the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States. Founded on October 28, 1636, by a vote of the Great and General Court of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, it was later named Harvard College in 1639 to honor its first major benefactor, Puritan clergyman John Harvard, who bequeathed half his estate and a library of over 400 books to the school.
     nstitutional Structure & Campus
     The university is divided into 14 distinct schools. Harvard College serves the undergraduate population (enrolling around 7,000 students), while 13 graduate and professional schools—including the renowned Harvard Business School, Harvard Medical School, and Harvard Law School—educate the postgraduate student body.
     While the historic heart of the university is Harvard Yard in Cambridge, the institution has expanded significantly with robust campuses across the Charles River in the Allston neighborhood of Boston, as well as the medical campus in Boston's Longwood area.
     Groundbreaking Research & Influence
     Harvard faculty and affiliated teaching hospitals are consistently at the forefront of global scientific breakthroughs. Notableongoing and recent achievements include:
     
     <u><b>Medical Advancements:</u></b> Pioneering research into stopping the progression of ALS, using lithium to prevent or reverse Alzheimer's pathology, discovering the GLP-1 hormone for diabetes treatments, and executing successful gene therapy clinical trials that restored hearing in deaf children.
